FFT: windowed spectrum. DCT: cosine coefficients. NUDFT: projections at nonuniform frequencies, including plate modes. Constant-Q: variable-window projections, limited by the available window. RFT: stored quantized 610-sample basis. These are complementary measurements, not five independent sources of energy. Values are digital dBFS, not calibrated physical vibration.

  

  

    What am I looking at?
    
A virtual square plate has resonant modes
       φ = cos(nπx)cos(mπy) − s·cos(mπx)cos(nπy) at f ≈ F₀(n²+m²). Each mode is
       driven by the sound energy near its own resonance, and the sand slides toward the
       nodal lines, as on a real Chladni plate. Loudness (dB) sets how hard the plate is driven.

    
The ear of the plate is the RFT: nodes fk = {(k+1)φ}, Φnk =
       N-1/2ei2πfkn, and the unitary
       U = Φ(Φ†Φ)-1/2, computed offline at Fibonacci dimensions
       (N = 1597 and 610 here; the desktop app uses 2584 and 987). Toggle to the plain FFT to compare.
       No advantage over the FFT is claimed: the finite construction is valid, and that's all this uses.
